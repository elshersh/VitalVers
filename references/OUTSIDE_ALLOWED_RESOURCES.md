# Resources Outside the Initial 5 Space Apps Challenge Links (`OUTSIDE_ALLOWED_RESOURCES.md`)

## 1. Space Apps 2026 Challenge Rules on Resource Usage

On the official challenge page (**R0**: `https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/`), the **Resources** tab lists five primary links:
- **R1**: NASA – 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight (`https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/`)
- **R2**: NASA – Risks of Human Spaceflight (`https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/`)
- **R3**: NASA – Ongoing HRP Research in Space (`https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research/`)
- **R4**: CSA – Monitoring Radiation for Astronaut Health (`https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp`)
- **R5**: CSA – Why Do We Conduct Science Experiments in Space? (`https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp`)

In addition, the **Objectives** section of **R0** explicitly states:
> *"You can use NASA's Human Research Program resources, along with the publicly available resources from other space agencies, to understand how spaceflight hazards affect human health, and to decide which indicators are useful and how they can support a quick assessment."*

However, the five overview pages (`R1–R5`) contain **only two numerical physiological parameters** (`1% to 1.5%` bone mass loss per month and `6 months` standard ISS mission duration, plus `~30%` neutron radiation fraction on the ISS). They contain **zero** numerical clinical ranges or alert thresholds for heart rate, blood pressure, blood glucose, SpO2, core body temperature, sleep hours, mood, or optic nerve sheath diameter (ONSD).

To avoid using unverified numbers quietly, every document outside the initial 5 links (`R1–R5`) is explicitly declared below, stored in `references/`, and verified by `tools/verify_sources.py`.

# NASA HRP: The 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight

**Source:** [NASA Human Research Program (HRP)](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/)

NASA's Human Research Program groups the physiological, operational, and psychological hazards of spaceflight into five interconnected categories (R-I-D-G-E).

## HAZ-01: Space Radiation (R)
- **Mechanism:** Beyond Earth's protective atmosphere and magnetosphere, astronauts are bombarded by ionizing radiation, primarily Galactic Cosmic Rays (GCR - high-energy atomic nuclei) and Solar Particle Events (SPE - energetic protons from solar flares).
### Physiological & Clinical Effects
  - Increased lifetime risk of radiation-induced carcinogenesis (leukemia, solid tumors)
  - Central Nervous System (CNS) alterations: cognitive impairment, executive function deficits, neuro-inflammation
  - Degenerative cardiovascular disease and endothelial tissue damage
  - Acute Radiation Sickness (ARS) risk during severe, unshielded SPEs (nausea, vomiting, immune collapse)
### Monitoring & Telemetry Needs
  - Personal radiation dosimeters (accumulated dose in mSv)
  - Real-time dose rate (mSv/hr or microGy/hr)
  - Neutron fraction detection (e.g., CANS instrument)
  - Biomarkers: DNA double-strand break repair indicators, complete blood counts (lymphocytes)
### Countermeasures & Mitigation
  - Storm shelters within deep-space transit vehicles
  - Hydrogen-rich active/passive shielding (polyethylene, water walls)
  - Radioprotective pharmaceuticals and antioxidant supplementation
  - Mission duration limits based on career permissible exposure limits (PEL)

## HAZ-02: Isolation and Confinement (I)
- **Mechanism:** Small multi-cultural crews residing in cramped, high-workload habitats for months to years, cut off from terrestrial social contact, natural diurnal light cycles, and familiar environments.
### Physiological & Clinical Effects
  - Behavioral health decline: depression, anxiety, chronic situational stress
  - Circadian desynchronization and chronic sleep debt (<6 hours/night)
  - Interpersonal tension, communication breakdowns, and psychological friction
  - Decreased alertness, cognitive processing speed, and situational awareness
### Monitoring & Telemetry Needs
  - Daily sleep duration (hours) and sleep architecture (REM/deep sleep via actigraphy)
  - Daily subjective mood rating (e.g., scale 1-10 or multidimensional mood score)
  - Cognitive assessment battery scores (reaction time, working memory)
  - Voice/speech acoustic stress analysis and facial expression metrics
### Countermeasures & Mitigation
  - Circadian-aligned dynamic habitat lighting (blue-enriched morning light)
  - Structured private communication with family and ground psychological support
  - Gamified psychological decompression and behavioral self-help software
  - Routine schedules balancing exercise, duties, and off-duty recreation

## HAZ-03: Distance from Earth (D)
- **Mechanism:** Transit to deep space (Moon ~240,000 miles; Mars ~140 million miles average). Communication latency increases up to 20-22 minutes each way for Mars, making real-time ground guidance impossible. Evacuation/resupply impossible.
### Physiological & Clinical Effects
  - Inability to evacuate ill or injured crew members to terrestrial emergency rooms
  - Exacerbation of acute medical events (appendicitis, sepsis, toxic exposure, fractures)
  - Heightened psychological burden on crew who must act as primary caregivers and flight surgeons
### Monitoring & Telemetry Needs
  - Autonomous onboard medical checklist completion logs
  - Consumable medical kit inventory tracking (pharmaceutical expiration, surgical tools)
  - Automated diagnostic sensor readings (ECG, ultrasound, point-of-care blood chemistry)
  - Clinical decision support system (CDSS) diagnostic confidence scores
### Countermeasures & Mitigation
  - Autonomous AI clinical decision support tools providing step-by-step diagnostic workflows
  - Point-of-care laboratory devices (i-STAT, compact hematology analyzers)
  - Cross-training crew members in emergency medical procedures and minor surgery
  - Rigorous telemedicine protocols optimized for store-and-forward communication

## HAZ-04: Gravity Fields (Altered Gravity) (G)
- **Mechanism:** Transitions through three gravitational states: 1G (Earth) -> 0G (Microgravity in transit) -> 0.16G (Lunar surface) / 0.38G (Martian surface) -> 1G return.
### Physiological & Clinical Effects
  - Bone mineral density loss: 1% to 1.5% loss per month in load-bearing bones (femur, spine, pelvis)
  - Muscle atrophy: loss of muscle mass, volume, and peak power, particularly in slow-twitch postural muscles
  - Cephalad fluid shift: ~2 liters of blood and interstitial fluid shift toward the chest and head, causing facial puffiness, nasal congestion, and decreased total plasma volume
  - Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS): optic disc edema, posterior globe flattening, hyperopic shift, and choroidal folds
  - Cardiovascular deconditioning and orthostatic intolerance upon return to gravity (dizziness, fainting)
  - Vestibular disorientation and Space Motion Sickness (SMS) during initial 48-72 hours
### Monitoring & Telemetry Needs
  - Blood pressure (systolic/diastolic mmHg, supine and standing/loaded)
  - Heart rate (resting, peak exercise bpm)
  - Body mass / weight tracking (using inertial acceleration mass measurement devices)
  - Ultrasound imaging: optic nerve sheath diameter (ONSD), cardiac ventricular volume
  - Muscle strength / dynamometry and jump power metrics
### Countermeasures & Mitigation
  - 2 to 2.5 hours of daily intense resistive and aerobic exercise (ARED, T2 treadmill, CEVIS cycle ergometer)
  - Lower Body Negative Pressure (LBNP) suits to draw fluids back to lower extremities
  - Fluid and electrolyte replenishment prior to gravitational re-entry
  - Compression garments (anti-g suits) for re-entry and post-landing mobility

## HAZ-05: Hostile Closed Environments (E)
- **Mechanism:** An engineered life-support habitat recycling 100% of air and water, with restricted volume, artificial atmospheric pressure and gas mixtures, continuous equipment acoustic noise, and altered microbial biomes.
### Physiological & Clinical Effects
  - Hypercapnia symptoms from localized carbon dioxide pockets (>3-5 mmHg CO2): headaches, lethargy, mental fog
  - Immune dysregulation: suppression of T-cell function and reactivation of latent herpesviruses (EBV, VZV)
  - Microbiome shifts and microbial proliferation in humid crevices, filters, and surfaces
  - Toxic inhalation risks from off-gassing synthetic polymers or chemical coolant leaks (e.g., ammonia)
  - Acoustic trauma and temporary threshold shifts from continuous environmental ECLSS fans/pumps
### Monitoring & Telemetry Needs
  - Ambient cabin CO2 partial pressure (mmHg) and total cabin pressure (psi / kPa)
  - Pulse Oximetry (SpO2 %)
  - Core body temperature (°C / °F) to detect early microbial infection
  - Cabin acoustic sound levels (dB)
  - Air and water microbial sample counts (CFU/m3, CFU/100mL)
### Countermeasures & Mitigation
  - Environmental Control and Life Support Systems (ECLSS) with catalytic oxidizers and CO2 scrubbers
  - High-Efficiency Particulate Air (HEPA) and microbial air filters
  - Regular habitat surface wiping with antimicrobial disinfectants
  - Strict materials qualification avoiding toxic volatile organic compound (VOC) off-gassing

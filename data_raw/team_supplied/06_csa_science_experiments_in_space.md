# CSA: Why Do We Conduct Science Experiments in Space?

- **URL:** [https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp)
- **Date Modified:** 2025-10-28

## Primary Rationale
- Prepare for deep-space destinations (Moon and Mars)
- Use knowledge obtained in microgravity to improve quality of life and medicine on Earth

## Physiological Insights & Earth Benefits
- **Accelerated Aging Analogy:** Living in weightless conditions alters the human body in ways closely resembling accelerated biological aging and health problems caused by extreme sedentary lifestyles. Studying astronauts in space for 6 months yields physiological adaptation data that would take years to observe on Earth.
- **Organ Systems Affected:** ['Bones: rapid demineralization, loss of trabecular architecture', 'Cardiovascular system: cardiac muscle deconditioning, arterial stiffness, fluid redistribution', 'Brain & balance: vestibular disruption, sensorimotor adaptation, neuro-vestibular reweighting', 'Metabolism: reduced insulin sensitivity and glucose handling']
- **Terrestrial Disease Applications:** ['Balance problems and fall risks in elderly seniors', 'Osteoporosis and bone fracture prevention', 'Cardiovascular disorders and arterial aging', 'Type-2 diabetes and metabolic insulin resistance']
- **Iss Experiment Scale:** On average, approximately 200 international science experiments are conducted aboard the ISS at any given time.

## Sub-Resource 1: Embedded Infographic (Verbatim Organ Breakdown)
### Blood
Bone marrow blood cell production affected; reduced RBCs cause anemia; low WBC count leaves body vulnerable to infection and increases radiation sensitivity.

### Radiation
Higher radiation doses cause cataracts, DNA damage, and increased cancer risk.

### Brain
Perception and orientation confusion; misinterpreting direction and speed of movement; space sickness.

### Heart And Blood Vessels
Blood vessels stiffen and age faster; development of insulin resistance leading toward Type 2 diabetes; increased cardiovascular disease risk.

### Muscles And Nervous System
Loss of muscle mass and strength; slower reflexes; exercise is less effective in space.

### Bones
Loss of density and strength without weight bearing; while adults over 50 lose ~1% per year on Earth, astronauts can lose up to 1.5% bone mass per month in space.

## Main Article Verbatim Extract
Science is our quest for more knowledge about the world and ourselves. For thousands of years, humanity has pushed boundaries in its search to learn more about our place in the universe.

Canada's participation in the International Space Station (ISS) allows our scientists to access the unique space environment and conduct cutting-edge experiments aboard the orbiting laboratory to prepare for deep-space destinations and use the knowledge obtained to improve our quality of life on Earth.

Missions to the Moon and Mars mean longer trips and more risks for astronauts, including: more time in weightlessness, more exposure to radiation, longer periods of isolation very far from home, increased delays in communication with Earth, and less ability to rely on Earth for food, materials, and emergency medical assistance.

Studying the way our body changes in space helps us understand the impacts of reduced levels of physical activity and issues that affect older adult populations on Earth. Living in weightless conditions changes the human body in many ways. The effects observed in astronauts are similar to accelerated aging and health problems caused by a sedentary lifestyle. Studying the human body in space for six months gives us data that would take years to gather on Earth. New information gained in space contributes to improved quality of life on Earth.

Canadian scientists use space to study our bones, heart, blood vessels and brain. Their experiments have produced findings that can help people suffering from balance problems, such as seniors; osteoporosis; cardiovascular disorders; and Type-2 diabetes.

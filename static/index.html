Project overview · NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026

A personal health co-pilot for long missions beyond Earth

On long missions, astronauts carry much of the responsibility for spotting changes in their own health. Vitalverse gathers daily health indicators, compares every reading with the astronaut's own pre-flight baseline, and explains what changed and what to do next — right on board.

  • Personal baselines, not fixed limitsThirty pre-flight days define what "normal" means for each astronaut.
  • Explainable alertsEvery alert shows the readings behind it and the countermeasure to follow.
  • Ready for the communication delayWorks offline on board; escalates to the flight surgeon on Earth.

Four body systems

What spaceflight changes — and what we watch

Cardiovascular

In weightlessness, body fluids shift up toward the head and blood vessels adapt.

  • Heart rate
  • Blood pressure
  • SpO₂
  • Temperature

Bone & muscle

Weight-bearing bones lose on average 1–1.5% of their density each month in space.

  • Bone density
  • Muscle strength
  • Exercise sessions

Immune

In a closed spacecraft, stress hormones can rise and the immune system can change.

  • Immune index
  • Stress hormones
  • Temperature

Sleep & mind

Isolation, sleep loss and a shifted body clock can lower mood and performance.

  • Sleep
  • Mood
  • Mental-health check-in
  • Alertness test

Sources: NASA Human Research Program hazard pages (gravity fields, hostile/closed environments, isolation and confinement).
